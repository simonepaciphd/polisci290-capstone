# Workflow 09 — Polishing the draft

**When:** week 9, before Sun, Nov 22. **Class:** Session 9, presenting, visualizing, and communicating results. **Feeds:** the **Edit: polish** milestone, at least 20 pages, and your final presentation.

## Goal

Revise the whole paper in **focused passes**, from the argument down to the commas, so that the version you submit this week is one you'd be happy to hand in. The syllabus asks for one thing: polish the final draft.

## You produce

A polished draft of at least 20 pages that:

1. Has been revised in five passes: argument, evidence, structure, clarity, honesty.
2. Has consistent terminology, citations, and formatting: Times New Roman, 12 pt, double-spaced, one citation style.
3. Has figures and tables a reader can understand at a glance.
4. And, for yourself: a short list of what you'll still change for the final paper.

## Steps

1. **You.** Read the whole paper once, start to finish, without editing. Write down the three things that bother you most.
2. **With AI (optional).** Review the draft in passes. Paste in your full draft:

   ```
   You are a reviewer for an applied political science capstone. Here is my full draft.
   Do not rewrite it. Review it in five separate passes and report findings for each:
   1. Argument: is the central claim clear, and does every section serve it?
   2. Evidence: is every claim supported? Is any result misreported or overstated?
      Is causal language used only where the design supports it?
   3. Structure: are the sections in the right order, with transitions between them?
   4. Clarity: which sentences or paragraphs would an educated non-specialist struggle with? Is jargon defined?
   5. Honesty: what limitations are missing? Does the introduction promise more than the paper delivers?
   Finish with a prioritized list of the 5 most important revisions.
   ```

3. **You decide.** Choose which revisions to make, starting with the argument and evidence, before the surface. Fixing commas while a section doesn't serve the argument is wasted effort.
4. **You.** Revise, in your own words. Anything the AI rewrites or restyles that you keep must be disclosed; better, rewrite it yourself.
5. **You.** Work on your figures, using Session 9: one message per figure, a title that states the finding, labels a reader can read. The figures you fix now will go into your presentation.
6. **You.** Do the final mechanical pass: word count, formatting, every citation checked against the bibliography, every source verified, figure and table numbers in order.
7. **You.** Write 3–5 lines on what you'll still change for the final paper, including anything you expect to learn at the conference.

## Check before moving on

- [ ] Five passes done, argument first.
- [ ] Terminology, citations, and formatting are consistent.
- [ ] Every source is verified; no AI-invented citations.
- [ ] Figures state their finding and read on their own.
- [ ] At least 20 pages, in your own words, with an AI-use note attached.

## Common traps

- **Proofreading instead of revising.** Revision means rethinking the argument and the evidence, not just the sentences.
- **Fixing everything at once.** One pass per problem catches more.
- **AI-polished prose you didn't write.** It breaks the course policy, and it tends to erase your own voice and argument.
- **Over-editing into blandness.** Clear is not the same as lifeless.
