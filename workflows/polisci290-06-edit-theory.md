# Workflow 06 — Editing the argument

**When:** week 6, before Sun, Nov 1. **Class:** Session 6, strengthening analytical frameworks. **Feeds:** the **Edit: theory** milestone, at least 12 pages.

## Goal

Make the theoretical and analytical sections of your paper as strong as they can be: a **refined thesis**, a **complete literature review**, and an argument that has faced its **strongest counterargument**. The syllabus asks for three things: focus on the theoretical and analytical sections; refine the thesis; complete the literature review.

## You produce

A revised draft of at least 12 pages in which:

1. **The thesis** is updated to match what you now know from your preliminary results.
2. **The mechanism** is stated step by step, with its scope conditions: where and when it applies, and when it breaks down.
3. **The literature review** positions your project: who you build on, who you argue against, and what you add.
4. **The counterargument** (your foil) is stated at its strongest, and answered.

## Steps

1. **You.** Reread your thesis next to your preliminary results. Does the thesis still describe what you're finding? If not, revise it now: a thesis that matches the evidence is stronger than one that ignores it.
2. **You.** Find your foil, as in the Session 6 workshop. Write the strongest argument against your thesis in 3–4 sentences, the way its supporters would put it.
3. **With AI (optional).** Test the argument against the foil. Paste in your thesis, mechanism, and foil:

   ```
   You are a demanding political science reviewer. Here is my thesis, my mechanism, and the strongest
   counterargument I found.
   1. Tell me whether I've stated the counterargument at its strongest. If not, state it better.
   2. Tell me what my argument and the counterargument predict differently. Where would the evidence
      tell them apart?
   3. Point out the conditions under which my mechanism would not work (scope conditions),
      and any assumption I'm making without saying so.
   4. Use counterfactual reasoning (Fearon 1991): if my cause had been absent, what does my argument say
      would have happened, and is that plausible?
   5. Tell me the single weakest point in my argument.
   ```

4. **You decide.** Decide how you'll answer the foil: with evidence, with scope conditions, or by conceding part of it. Decide the scope of your claim.
5. **You.** Complete the literature review. For each source, ask what it does for your argument. Cut sources that do nothing, and look for what's missing: the strongest critics of your position, and the study your design most resembles. Verify every new source.
6. **You.** Revise the theoretical sections in your own words. Every paragraph should advance your argument.
7. **With AI (optional).** Review the revised sections:

   ```
   Here are the theory and literature sections of my capstone. Do not rewrite them.
   1. Mark every paragraph that summarizes literature without advancing my argument.
   2. Tell me whether each step of my mechanism is supported by a source, a logical argument, or neither.
   3. Tell me whether the counterargument is answered, or just mentioned.
   4. Check that my key terms are used the same way throughout.
   ```

## Check before moving on

- [ ] Your thesis matches what your evidence shows so far.
- [ ] Your mechanism has scope conditions and at least one condition under which it fails.
- [ ] The strongest counterargument is stated fairly and answered.
- [ ] Every source does a job, and every source is verified.
- [ ] At least 12 pages, in your own words, with an AI-use note attached.

## Common traps

- **A straw-man foil.** If you can't state the counterargument as its supporters would, you can't answer it.
- **Defending the week-2 thesis at all costs.** Refining the thesis is this week's job.
- **A literature review as a list.** "A found X. B found Y." is not a review. Say what each source means for your argument.
- **No scope conditions.** An argument that works everywhere, always, is usually too vague to be tested.
