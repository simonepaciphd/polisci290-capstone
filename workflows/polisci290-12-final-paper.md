# Workflow 12 — The final capstone paper

**When:** after the Final Research Conference, through finals week (exact date on Canvas). **Feeds:** the **final capstone paper** (40%): at least 5000 words, about 20 pages.

## Goal

Hand in the finished project: one integrated argument, with claims that match your evidence, **improved by what you learned at the conference**. Most of the work is done: this last round is about the feedback, the claims, and the details.

## You produce

- The final paper: at least **5000 words, about 20 pages**, not counting the bibliography, title page, and figures. Times New Roman, 12 pt, double-spaced, one consistent citation style.
- An **AI-use note** (`docs/polisci290-ai-use-and-disclosure.md`).

### A default structure (adapt it to your format)

| Section | Contains | Comes from |
|---|---|---|
| Introduction | The problem, your question, your answer in 2–3 sentences, why it matters to your audience | Project Question, week 8 framing |
| Argument | Your thesis, mechanism, scope, and the literature it builds on and argues against | Working Thesis, week 6 edit |
| Research design | Unit, cases, measures, sources, mode of analysis | Data Plan |
| Findings | What you found, with tables and figures | Preliminary Results, week 7 edit |
| Robustness and limitations | Checks, validity, what the design can't show | Week 7 edit |
| Conclusion | What it means, recommendations if your format calls for them, what comes next | Week 8 edit, conference feedback |

Strategy memos, green papers, white papers, and risk reports usually lead with a summary and recommendations, and put the design in a shorter section or an appendix. See `docs/polisci290-capstone-formats.md`.

## Steps

The paper you submit must be in your own words. AI can help you plan and review it, but it can't write it. Anything it restyles or rewrites that you keep must be disclosed.

1. **You.** Go through your notes from the conference and both progress reports. List every question or objection you heard, and mark each: *will address*, *already addressed*, or *out of scope* (with the reason).
2. **You decide.** Pick the revisions that matter most. The conference questions are a preview of how any reader will push back.
3. **You.** Revise, in your own words. Address the points you picked in the text, where a reader would raise them.
4. **With AI (optional).** Do a final review:

   ```
   You are a reviewer for a final applied political science capstone, written as a [format] for [audience].
   Here is my paper. Do not rewrite it.
   1. Tell me whether my answer is clear on the first page, and whether every section serves it.
   2. List my 5 most important claims. For each, compare the strength of the claim with the strength
      of the evidence. Flag overclaiming AND underclaiming.
   3. Tell me whether the strongest counterargument is addressed.
   4. Tell me whether the paper reads like one argument or like milestones stapled together.
   Finish with a prioritized list of the 3 most important last revisions.
   ```

5. **You decide.** Choose which revisions to make, and make them. Calibrate your claims: "causes" only if your design supports it, "is associated with" for correlation, description as description. Don't bury null results.
6. **You.** Final checks: word count, formatting, every citation verified and matched to the bibliography, figures numbered, AI-use note attached.

## Check before submitting

- [ ] Your answer appears on page one.
- [ ] Every claim is supported, and causal language only where the design supports it.
- [ ] The strongest counterargument is addressed.
- [ ] Feedback from the conference and the progress reports is addressed.
- [ ] Every citation is verified; no AI-invented sources.
- [ ] At least 5000 words; Times New Roman 12 pt, double-spaced; one citation style.
- [ ] The AI-use note is attached.

## Common traps

- **Ignoring the conference.** The questions you got are the best free review you'll ever get.
- **Last-minute new analyses.** Finish and report what you have; new work this late tends to break what already works.
- **Hedging everything, or nothing.** Make each claim exactly as strong as its evidence.
- **AI-polished prose you didn't write.** This breaks the course policy.
