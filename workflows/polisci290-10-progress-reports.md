# Workflow 10 — Progress reports

**When:** twice during the quarter. One is **planned**: you pick the date through the signup survey at the start of the quarter. One is **random**: names are drawn at the beginning of each session, so be ready from week 2 on. **Feeds:** the progress reports grade (5%), and the milestone you're working on.

## Goal

Use five to ten minutes of the class's attention to **get feedback on one decision** you're facing. A progress report isn't a performance: it's a chance to discuss a challenge or a doubt, and leave with better options than you came with.

| | Planned progress report | Random-draw progress report |
|---|---|---|
| When | The date you signed up for | Any session: names drawn at the start |
| Length | 5–10 minutes with Q&A | 5–10 minutes with Q&A |
| Slides | 1–2 slides | No slides |
| Focus | The weekly milestone, or one specific aspect of your project | The same |
| What you do | Discuss one analytical or empirical choice, and ask the class for feedback | The same |

## You produce

- **One choice**: the analytical or empirical decision you want feedback on, stated in one sentence. For example: *"Should I measure media access by broadband coverage or by local newspaper circulation?"*
- **The options**: two or three ways you could go, and what each would mean for your project.
- **One question for the class**: what you most want them to tell you.
- **For the planned report:** 1–2 slides.
- **After the report:** a note of the feedback you got, and what you'll do with it.

## Steps

1. **You.** Pick the choice. The best ones are real: a decision you haven't made yet, where the options lead to different projects. Keep a running note of open choices each week, so you're ready for the random draw.
2. **You.** In 30 seconds, say what your project is: the question, and the thesis. Then state the choice and your options.
3. **With AI (optional).** Sharpen the choice before class. Paste in your latest milestone and the choice:

   ```
   I'm presenting a 5-10 minute progress report on my political science capstone to my seminar,
   to get feedback on one decision. Here is my latest milestone and the decision I'm facing.
   1. Tell me whether I've stated the decision clearly, and whether my options are really different.
   2. For each option, tell me what it would gain and cost for my project.
   3. Suggest the one question I should ask the class to get the most useful feedback.
   Do not make the decision for me.
   ```

4. **You.** For the planned report, make 1–2 slides: the project in one line, the choice and the options. For the random draw, practice saying it without slides.
5. **You.** Present. Then listen: write down what you hear, especially the objections.
6. **You decide.** After class, make the choice. Write a short note of the feedback and what you decided, and use it in your next milestone.

## Check before moving on

- [ ] You can state the choice in one sentence.
- [ ] You have two or three real options, and one question for the class.
- [ ] You can describe your project in 30 seconds without slides.
- [ ] After the report, you've written down the feedback and your decision.

## Common traps

- **A status update instead of a choice.** "Here's what I did this week" gets you little feedback.
- **A choice you've already made.** If you're not open to the answer, the class can't help.
- **Too much background.** Thirty seconds on the project is enough; spend the rest on the choice.
- **Not being ready for the draw.** Keep one open choice in your pocket every week.
