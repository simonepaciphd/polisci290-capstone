# Workflow 05 — The rough draft

**When:** week 5, before Sun, Oct 25. **Class:** Session 5, framing and selling a project. **Feeds:** the **Rough Draft** milestone, at least 10 pages. From here on, you work on one paper.

## Goal

Turn four milestones into **one paper**: pick your format and audience, build the structure, and give every piece of your work a home. The syllabus asks for three things: combine all previous milestones; structure the final project paper; integrate and expand each section.

## You produce

A rough draft of at least 10 pages with:

1. **A structure** that fits your format (see `docs/polisci290-capstone-formats.md`), with a heading for every section.
2. **Every earlier milestone in its place**, expanded: the question in the introduction, the thesis and mechanism in the argument section, the data plan in the design section, the preliminary results in the findings.
3. **A working introduction** that states the problem, your answer, and why it matters to your audience.
4. **Placeholders** for what's still missing, marked clearly (for example, *[robustness check, week 7]*).

## Steps

1. **You.** Choose your format and your audience, if you haven't yet. Who is the reader: a scholar, a campaign manager, a legislator's staff, a firm's board, an editor? What do they need to decide or understand? Read `docs/polisci290-capstone-formats.md`.
2. **You.** Write your value proposition in two sentences, as in Session 5: what does your project give this audience that they don't already have?
3. **With AI (optional).** Explore structures. Paste in your four milestones, your format, and your audience:

   ```
   You are an editor helping me structure a 20-page applied political science capstone.
   My format is [format] and my audience is [audience]. Here are my four milestones so far.
   1. Suggest 2 different structures for the paper, as section headings with one line each on what goes there.
      One should follow the conventions of my format; the other can depart from them.
   2. For each structure, tell me where each of my milestones goes, and what is still missing.
   3. Suggest 2 ways to frame the project for my audience (what problem it solves for them),
      and say which of my findings each framing puts first.
   Do not write any of the text.
   ```

4. **You decide.** Settle your structure and your framing. Then build the annotated structure from the Session 5 workshop: under every heading, write 2–4 bullets on what goes there. Every piece of evidence should have a section. Nothing should be left without a home.
5. **You.** Draft section by section, in your own words. Start with the section you know best. Expand each milestone: the 350-word versions become full sections. Mark what's missing instead of padding.
6. **You.** Write a working introduction last: the problem, your answer in 2–3 sentences, why it matters to your audience, and a road map.
7. **With AI (optional).** Check your draft before you submit:

   ```
   Here is my rough draft and my annotated structure. Do not rewrite it.
   1. Tell me whether each section does the job my structure gives it, and where the draft drifts.
   2. Tell me whether the introduction states my answer, and whether the rest of the draft delivers it.
   3. List what is missing or thin, in order of importance.
   ```

## Check before moving on

- [ ] You can name your format and your audience.
- [ ] Every earlier milestone has a place in the draft, expanded.
- [ ] The introduction states your answer, not just your topic.
- [ ] Missing pieces are marked, with the week you'll fill them.
- [ ] At least 10 pages, in your own words, with an AI-use note attached.

## Common traps

- **Stapling milestones together.** Each milestone was written to stand alone; the paper has to read as one argument.
- **The format as a costume.** A strategy memo isn't a research paper with a different title. Its structure and tone follow its reader.
- **Padding to reach 10 pages.** Expand what you have; mark what's missing.
- **A literature review that runs the paper.** Organize the paper around your argument, not around your reading list.
