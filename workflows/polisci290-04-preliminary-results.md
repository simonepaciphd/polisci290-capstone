# Workflow 04 — Preliminary results



**When:** week 4, before Sun, Oct 18. **Class:** Session 4, implementing empirical analysis. **Feeds:** the **Preliminary Results** milestone, at least 350 words, and later the findings section of your paper.

## Goal

Run the first round of your analysis, and say **honestly what it shows**, and what kind of evidence it is. The syllabus asks for two things: implement the proposed data analysis; present and discuss preliminary results.

## You produce

A Preliminary Results milestone (at least 350 words) with:

1. **What you ran**: the analysis from your data plan, in 2–3 sentences, and any change you made to the plan, with the reason.
2. **What you found**: your first key result, with a table or figure if it helps.
3. **What kind of evidence it is**: *suggestive*, *descriptive*, *predictive*, or *causal* (Session 4), and what that lets you claim.
4. **What it means for your expectations**: supported, contradicted, or unclear.
5. **What's next**: the analysis or check you'll add in week 7.

## Steps

1. **You.** Look at your data before you test anything. Count your observations, check for missing values, and look at the distribution of your key variables. Write down anything surprising.
2. **With AI (optional).** Get the smallest thing working first: one table, one chart, one case coded. AI can help you write and debug code, clean data, and set up the analysis you planned. **You** check what it produces: look at a few cases you know, and make sure the numbers make sense. **Save your AI chats**: you'll need them for your AI-use note.
3. **With AI (optional).** Run the main analysis from your data plan. Run what you planned, before you try variations.
4. **You.** Before you look at anything else, write 2–3 sentences on what the result says about your main expectation. Your first reading, before you start building a story, is often the most honest one.
5. **With AI (optional).** Interpret the result, as in the Session 4 "pass, pair, and critique" workshop. Paste in your expectations, your result, and your first reading:

   ```
   You are a skeptical reader of an undergraduate political science capstone. Here are my expectations,
   my first key result (with the table or figure), and my own interpretation.
   1. Tell me what kind of evidence this is: suggestive, descriptive, predictive, or causal,
      and what claim it can support. Point out any causal language my design can't back up.
   2. Tell me whether the result actually speaks to my expectation, or to something else.
   3. Name the 2 most likely alternative explanations for this result.
   4. If the result is null or goes the "wrong" way, help me tell whether it is a real finding
      or a problem with the data or the code. Do not suggest changes designed to make the result significant.
   5. Suggest the single most useful next analysis or check.
   ```

6. **You decide.** Settle what you'll claim from this result, and what you'll add next. A result that goes against your thesis is a finding: report it.
7. **You.** Write the milestone in your own words. Report what the evidence shows before you say what it means.

## Check before moving on

- [ ] The milestone reports an analysis you actually ran, not one you plan to run.
- [ ] You've checked the AI-assisted code or output on cases you know.
- [ ] You can name the kind of evidence, and your language matches it.
- [ ] Your result is connected to a specific expectation.
- [ ] Your output (tables, figures, code) is saved and labelled.
- [ ] At least 350 words, in your own words, with an AI-use note attached.

## Common traps

- **Presenting plans as results.** The syllabus asks you to *implement* the analysis.
- **Tweaking until it's significant.** Adding controls or dropping cases until the result looks good isn't analysis. Report what you planned, and label anything else as exploratory.
- **"Causes" from a correlation.** Match the verb to the evidence: "is associated with" is often the honest one.
- **Trusting code that runs.** Code that runs isn't the same as code that's right.
- **Losing your output.** A result that lives only in a chat window or a console is a result you'll have to redo.
