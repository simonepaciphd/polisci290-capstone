# Workflow 07 — Editing the evidence

**When:** week 7, before Sun, Nov 8. **Class:** Session 7, empirical validity and evidence robustness. **Feeds:** the **Edit: empirics** milestone, at least 15 pages.

## Goal

Finish your analysis, find out whether it **holds up**, and say clearly **what it can't show**. The syllabus asks for three things: focus on the empirical sections; conclude the data analysis; discuss robustness and limitations.

## You produce

A revised draft of at least 15 pages in which:

1. **The analysis is complete**: every expectation from your data plan has a result, reported as supported, contradicted, or unclear.
2. **Two or three robustness checks** target the real weak points of your main result, with the standard for passing set in advance.
3. **Validity is discussed**: the main threats to *internal* validity (is the relationship real, in your cases?) and *external* validity (does it travel beyond them?).
4. **Limitations are stated**, separate from scope: what your design can't show, and why.
5. **Tables and figures** are clean, labelled, and readable on their own.

## Steps

1. **You.** List every expectation from your data plan and mark each: *result in hand*, *analysis still to run*, or *dropped* (with the reason). Run what's missing first.
2. **You decide.** Choose **two or three checks**. Pick the things a skeptic would attack first, and decide *before* each check what would count as passing. For example:
   - **Measurement:** does the result survive a different indicator for your key variable?
   - **Sample:** does it survive dropping unusual cases, or a different time period?
   - **Rival explanation:** does it hold when you account for the foil's explanation?
   - **Qualitative evidence:** does the account hold if you drop your weakest source, or read the same evidence another way?
3. **With AI (optional).** Design a secondary empirical test, as in the Session 7 workshop. Paste in your thesis, design, and main result:

   ```
   You are a methods reviewer for an undergraduate political science capstone. Here is my thesis,
   my research design, and my main result.
   1. Name the 3 most serious threats to internal validity (confounders, reverse causation, selection,
      measurement), in order of seriousness, for my specific design.
   2. For each, suggest a check I could realistically run this week with my data, and what result would
      count as passing.
   3. Suggest one secondary empirical design that tests the same thesis a different way.
   4. Tell me how far my result can travel: to which other places, periods, or groups, and why.
   ```

4. **With AI (optional).** Run the checks. AI can help you set them up and run them. Whether a check passed is your call, against the standard you set in advance. Save all output.
5. **You.** Build your tables and figures: descriptive labels (not variable names), informative titles, a note on the data source. A reader should understand each one without reading the text. Put secondary results in an appendix.
6. **You.** Revise the empirical sections in your own words. Report what each analysis shows before you say what it means. Then write the limitations: what you can't show, and why it matters.
7. **With AI (optional).** Review the empirical sections:

   ```
   Here are the empirical sections of my capstone, with my tables and figures. Do not rewrite them.
   1. List my 5 most important empirical claims. For each, compare the strength of the claim
      with the strength of the evidence. Flag overclaiming AND underclaiming.
   2. Tell me whether every expectation from my data plan is addressed.
   3. Tell me whether my robustness checks address the real threats, or only easy ones.
   4. Tell me whether any table or figure can't be understood on its own.
   ```

## Check before moving on

- [ ] Every expectation has a result, including nulls and contradictions.
- [ ] Two or three targeted checks, each with a pass standard set in advance.
- [ ] Internal and external validity are both discussed.
- [ ] Limitations are stated plainly.
- [ ] Tables and figures stand on their own.
- [ ] At least 15 pages, in your own words, with an AI-use note attached.

## Common traps

- **Checks for show.** Three checks aimed at real threats beat ten that aren't.
- **Deciding what "passing" means after you see the result.**
- **Hiding a null.** An expectation that isn't supported is a finding. Report it clearly.
- **Limitations as an afterthought.** A short, honest limitations section makes the rest of your claims more credible.
- **Raw output as tables.** Console printouts don't belong in the paper.
