# POLISCI 290 Capstone Project — Overview



## The project in one paragraph

You write an **applied research project**: you identify a **real-world problem or puzzle**, formulate a **thesis, argument, or actionable strategy** that draws on political science, and support your claims with **empirical analysis**. You choose the field and the format, from a research paper to a strategy memo or a risk report, the way political science is used in professional settings (see `docs/polisci290-capstone-formats.md`). For graduating seniors, the project is the capstone of the major: it pulls together what you've learned across your coursework.

The capstone is an **individual project**.

## What a good project looks like

- **A specific question.** Not a topic. "Immigration and elections" is a topic. "Did county-level exposure to ICE raids in 2018 change Latino turnout in the following midterm?" is a question.
- **A clear claim.** You propose an answer, a strategy, or a solution, and you can say why it holds (the mechanism).
- **Grounded in political science.** Your argument draws on the scientific literature, not only on news and opinion.
- **Real evidence.** A concrete measurement strategy, data you can actually get, and an analysis that speaks to your claim.
- **Honest about limits.** Your claims are only as strong as your evidence.
- **Written for its audience.** A strategy memo reads like a strategy memo; a research paper reads like a research paper.

## Milestones and deadlines

Deadlines are 11:59 p.m. unless stated.

You write the project one piece at a time, with a milestone due every Sunday. The early milestones each draft one section of the final paper. The later ones expand, edit, and polish the full draft.

| Component | Due | What it is | Minimum length |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Project Question** | Sun, Sep 27 | Motivate the project; identify a question, puzzle, or problem; use clear theoretical variables | 250 words |
| **Working Thesis** | Sun, Oct 4 | Propose an answer, strategy, or solution; draw on the political science literature; describe the mechanisms behind the thesis | 350 words, 5 citations |
| **Data Plan** | Sun, Oct 11 | Propose a concrete measurement strategy; identify and describe accessible data sources; discuss the mode of analysis | 350 words |
| **Preliminary Results** | Sun, Oct 18 | Implement the proposed analysis; present and discuss preliminary results | 350 words |
| **Rough Draft** | Sun, Oct 25 | Combine all previous milestones; structure the paper; integrate and expand each section | 10 pages |
| **Edit: theory** | Sun, Nov 1 | Focus on the theoretical and analytical sections; refine the thesis; complete the literature review | 12 pages |
| **Edit: empirics** | Sun, Nov 8 | Focus on the empirical sections; conclude the analysis; discuss robustness and limitations | 15 pages |
| **Full final draft** | Sun, Nov 15 | Focus on the introduction and conclusion; finalize the framing of the project | 20 pages |
| **Edit: polish** | Sun, Nov 22 | Polish the final draft | 20 pages |
| Progress reports | Two during the quarter (one by signup, one by random draw) | Short in-class presentations on one choice you're making | 5–10 minutes with Q&A |
| **Final research presentation** | Mon, Nov 30 / Wed, Dec 2 | Final Research Conference, in class | 10 minutes, plus Q&A |
| **Final capstone paper** | Finals week (exact date on Canvas) | The finished project | At least 5000 words (about 20 pages) |

**Only your best 6 milestone grades count.** But 3 points are taken off your total milestone points for each milestone you don't submit, so submit all nine.

**Office hours.** You must attend at least two office hours to discuss your progress: one early in the quarter and one toward the end. Book them through the sign-up link in the syllabus.

## The final paper: format rules

- At least **5000 words, about 20 pages**, not counting the bibliography, title page, and figures.
- Times New Roman, 12 pt, double-spaced.
- Any citation style (MLA, Chicago, APA…), as long as it's consistent.
- An AI-use note attached (see `docs/polisci290-ai-use-and-disclosure.md`). The same goes for every milestone.

## How the pieces connect

```
Question ─► Thesis ─► Data Plan ─► Results ─► Rough Draft ─► Edits (theory, empirics, framing, polish) ─► Presentation ─► Final paper
  Sep 27     Oct 4      Oct 11      Oct 18       Oct 25          Nov 1 · Nov 8 · Nov 15 · Nov 22         Nov 30/Dec 2    finals week
  wf 01      wf 02      wf 03       wf 04        wf 05           wf 06 · wf 07 · wf 08 · wf 09            wf 11           wf 12
```

The first four milestones each become a section of your paper: the question becomes the introduction, the thesis the argument section, the data plan the research design, and the preliminary results the first version of your findings. From week 5 on, you work on one paper that grows from 10 to 20 pages. Nothing you write is thrown away. Workflow 10 covers the two progress reports, which can come at any point in the quarter.
