

# AI use and disclosure

## The rule (syllabus, verbatim)

> Depending on the context, artificial intelligence (AI) can either enhance and impede learning. There are times when these tools might provide us with new ideas and understandings (e.g., brainstorming or getting feedback). At other points, practicing skills and synthesizing ideas on our own will be crucial for the learning process. Thus, being mindful of when to use these tools will help us navigate the complexity of interacting with AI technologies in the classroom and beyond.
>
> In this class, we will use AI tools, including ChatGPT, that harness large language models as pedagogical opportunities for learning and teaching in the course. Doing so will align with one of our course goals, which pertains to the evaluation of arguments and justification with evidence.
>
> For this class, all assignments should be written in your own words. Just as you cannot take credit for others’ writing in your assignments, you cannot use paraphrasing software (“spinbots”) or AI writing software (like ChatGPT) and submit the output as your own. Doing so in this is a violation of the Stanford Honor Code.

On top of the syllabus rule, **every milestone and the final paper need an AI-use note** (template below). A missing note costs 5% of that component's grade.

Everything below is practical guidance on following these rules. If anything here seems to conflict with the syllabus, the syllabus wins.

## What that means for each part of the project

| Part of the project | AI use | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Brainstorming and stress-testing your question, thesis, design, or argument | **Encouraged**, disclose | The AI suggests options; you choose. Use the prompts in the workflows |
| Feedback on your drafts | **Encouraged**, disclose | Ask it to review, not to rewrite |
| Finding source leads | Allowed, disclose | Verify every source yourself; see below |
| Code, data cleaning, and running analyses | Allowed, disclose | Check what it produces on cases you know |
| Charts and figures | Allowed, disclose | You decide what each figure shows |
| **The text you submit** (every milestone, the final paper) | **Must be in your own words** | The AI may not write it, paraphrase it, or restyle it for you |
| Slides and presentation practice | Allowed, disclose | You deliver the talk yourself |

## Suggested practices

We recommend these, but they're **suggestions, not requirements**. The binding rules are the syllabus policy quoted above and the AI-use note.

### Human first, then AI

1. **Pass 1, you.** Make a real first attempt in your own words: a question, a thesis, a data plan, a draft.
2. **Pass 2, AI with control.** Then use AI to challenge, clarify, or extend it.
3. **Keep the thread.** You should always be able to say what was yours before AI, and what changed after.

This makes your AI-use note easy to write, and it protects the learning the capstone is for.

### Privacy

Don't paste private, confidential, or identifying material into AI tools. That includes raw interview notes, non-anonymized data, and classmates' unpublished work.

## Verification (non-negotiable in practice)

- **AI invents citations.** Before any source goes into your work, check that it exists, that it's cited accurately, and that it says what you use it for.
- **AI isn't evidence.** Don't cite an AI system for facts about politics, law, policy, or public opinion.
- **You own the errors.** Anything under your name is your responsibility, including mistakes you didn't catch.

## What to disclose

**Disclose** when AI meaningfully shaped:

- your question, thesis, argument, or structure;
- your analysis, code, or data;
- your source search;
- your figures or slides.

**No disclosure needed** for spell-check, grammar fixes, formatting, or a synonym look-up that leaves your phrasing intact.

**When in doubt, disclose.**

## AI-use note template (suggested format)

Attach one to every milestone and to the final paper: a short section at the end of your document works. If you didn't use AI for a submission, say so in one line.

```
AI-USE NOTE — [milestone name / Final paper]

Tools used: [e.g., model name and version, Stanford AI Playground]

What I used AI for:
- [Task] — [what the AI did] — [what I kept, changed, or rejected]
- [Task] — ...

What is entirely mine: [e.g., the question, the thesis, all the prose]

Verification: [e.g., all 7 sources checked on Google Scholar; code output checked against 20 cases by hand]
```

**Example entry:**
"Stress-testing my thesis: the AI suggested that local media closures might matter through party recruitment rather than voter information. I kept my mechanism, but added the recruitment pathway as a rival explanation in section 3."

## Access

You have access to frontier AI models through Stanford, and to the Stanford AI Playground. If cost or access is ever a barrier for a particular task, contact the instructor.
