

# Rubrics

Each graded part of the project has a rubric below. Each criterion is scored on three levels: **Strong (2 points)**, **Developing (1)**, or **Weak (0)**. All criteria in a component count equally, so your grade for a component is the points you earn divided by the maximum. For example, a Data Plan scored 2, 2, 1 earns 5 of 6 points.

## Applies to every component

- **Your own words.** All assignments must be written in your own words (syllabus AI policy). Submitting AI-written text as your own is an Honor Code matter, not a rubric deduction.
- **AI-use note attached.** Every milestone and the final paper need an AI-use note (see `docs/polisci290-ai-use-and-disclosure.md`). A missing note costs **5%** of that component's grade.
- **Minimum length.** Each milestone, and the final paper, has a minimum length (see `polisci290-00-project-overview.md`). Falling short costs **5%** of that component's grade.
- **Best 6 of 9.** Only your best 6 milestone grades count, and each milestone you don't submit costs 3 points from your milestone total.

## Milestones (5% each)

*Syllabus: each criterion below is one of the milestone's stated expectations.*

### Project Question (250 words)

| Criterion | Strong (2) | Developing (1) | Weak (0) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Motivation** | A concrete reason the question matters (a number, a named gap, a named audience); passes "so what?" | Reasons given, but generic ("this is important") | No clear reason |
| **Question, puzzle, or problem** | Specific and answerable; who, where, when, and what outcome are clear; doesn't assume its answer | A real question, but still broad or vague in one dimension | A topic rather than a question |
| **Theoretical variables** | Key variables named, distinct, and defined | Variables named but vague or overlapping | No clear variables |

### Working Thesis (350 words, 5 citations)

| Criterion | Strong (2) | Developing (1) | Weak (0) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Answer, strategy, or solution** | A clear, contestable claim that answers the question | A claim, but vague or too safe | A topic, not a claim |
| **Political science literature** | At least 5 verified sources, each doing a job for the argument, including a competing view | Sources present but listed rather than used, or no competing view | Fewer than 5, or not political science, or unverifiable |
| **Mechanisms** | Step-by-step mechanism with actors and institutions; no black boxes | Mechanism sketched, with gaps between cause and effect | No mechanism |

### Data Plan (350 words)

| Criterion | Strong (2) | Developing (1) | Weak (0) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Measurement strategy** | Each key variable has a concrete indicator; what it misses is acknowledged | Indicators named but loosely tied to the concepts | No concrete measures |
| **Data sources** | Accessible sources identified and described (coverage, unit, access), with a backup | Sources named, but access or coverage unchecked | No accessible source |
| **Mode of analysis** | The analysis and its comparison are clear and fit the question | Analysis named, but the comparison or fit is unclear | No analysis plan |

### Preliminary Results (350 words)

| Criterion | Strong (2) | Developing (1) | Weak (0) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Implementation** | The planned analysis was actually run; changes to the plan are explained | Partly run, or plans and results mixed together | Mostly plans |
| **Presentation and discussion** | Results shown clearly, tied to an expectation, and interpreted with language that matches the kind of evidence | Results shown, but the interpretation over- or under-claims | Results unclear or not interpreted |

### Rough Draft (10 pages)

| Criterion | Strong (2) | Developing (1) | Weak (0) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Combines previous milestones** | All four milestones are in the draft, revised to fit together | Milestones present but pasted in as they were | Milestones missing |
| **Structure** | A clear structure that fits the chosen format and audience | Structure present but generic or uneven | No clear structure |
| **Integration and expansion** | Each section expanded beyond its milestone; gaps marked | Some sections expanded | Little beyond the milestones |

### Edit: theory (12 pages)

| Criterion | Strong (2) | Developing (1) | Weak (0) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Theoretical and analytical sections** | Argue rather than summarize; counterargument stated fairly and answered | Mostly argued; counterargument weak or only mentioned | Mostly summary |
| **Refined thesis** | Thesis updated to fit the evidence so far, with scope conditions | Thesis revised but scope unclear | Unchanged despite evidence against it, or unclear |
| **Complete literature review** | Positions the project: allies, foils, and contribution; all sources verified | Coverage adequate but positioning thin | Incomplete or a list |

### Edit: empirics (15 pages)

| Criterion | Strong (2) | Developing (1) | Weak (0) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Empirical sections** | Clear design and findings; tables and figures stand on their own | Mostly clear; some tables or figures hard to read | Hard to follow |
| **Concluded data analysis** | Every expectation addressed, including nulls | Most expectations addressed | Analysis incomplete |
| **Robustness and limitations** | 2–3 targeted checks with standards set in advance; limitations stated plainly | Checks generic, or limitations brief | Missing |

### Full final draft (20 pages)

| Criterion | Strong (2) | Developing (1) | Weak (0) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Introduction and conclusion** | The answer is on page one; the conclusion delivers what the introduction promises | Present, but promise and delivery don't fully match | Missing, or mismatched |
| **Framing** | Framed for a clear audience; recommendations or implications tied to findings | Framing present but generic, or implications stretch the evidence | No clear framing |

### Edit: polish (20 pages)

| Criterion | Strong (2) | Developing (1) | Weak (0) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Polished final draft** | Clear, consistent, and formatted; citations complete and consistent | Minor problems of clarity, consistency, or formatting | Reads as unrevised |

## Progress reports (5%)

*Syllabus: focus on the weekly milestone or one aspect of the project; discuss one analytical or empirical choice and elicit feedback. 5–10 minutes with Q&A.*

Progress reports are graded on **completion**: give both of your reports. The table below isn't scored. It describes what a strong report looks like, so use it to prepare.

| Criterion | Strong | Developing | Weak |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The choice** | One real analytical or empirical choice, clearly stated, with options | A choice, but vague or already made | A status update, no choice |
| **Eliciting feedback** | Asks a clear question; engages with the answers | Some engagement | No engagement with the class |
| **Time and format** | Within 5–10 minutes; 1–2 slides (planned) or none (random draw) | Somewhat over or under | Well outside the format |

## Final research presentation (10%)

*Syllabus: pitch the project; illustrate the argument; illustrate the empirical strategy; discuss and visualize key results. 10 minutes plus Q&A, 5–10 slides.*

| Criterion | Strong (2) | Developing (1) | Weak (0) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pitch** | The motivating problem is clear and the audience knows why it matters | Problem stated, but the "why" is thin | No clear problem |
| **Argument** | Thesis, argument, or solution stated as one clear claim | A claim, but it gets lost | A topic, not a claim |
| **Empirical strategy** | Design, data sources, and analysis explained briefly and clearly | Explained, but hard to follow or too long | Missing |
| **Results** | Key results shown visually; limits stated; plans not presented as results | Results shown, but claims somewhat stronger than the evidence | Little evidence, or overclaiming |
| **Delivery and slides** | Within time; slides support the talk; one idea per slide | Somewhat over time or text-heavy | Well over time, or reads the slides |
| **Q&A** | Answers directly, including hard questions; acknowledges limits | Answers partly or defensively | Can't answer basic questions |

## Final capstone paper (40%)

*Syllabus: identify a real-world problem or question; apply theoretical and analytical insights from political science; employ an empirical methodology to support claims with data-driven evidence. At least 5000 words.*

| Criterion | Strong (2) | Developing (1) | Weak (0) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Problem and question** | A real-world problem, a specific question, and the answer on page one | Clear problem, but the question or answer is buried or broad | No clear question |
| **Political science insight** | Argument and mechanism built on the literature; counterargument answered | Literature used, but the argument or the counterargument is thin | Little political science |
| **Empirical methodology** | Design fits the question; measures, sources, and analysis explained well enough to check | Design mostly fits; gaps in explanation | Design can't answer the question |
| **Evidence and calibration** | Every major claim supported; causal language only where the design supports it; nulls reported; robustness and limits addressed | Mostly supported; some over- or under-claiming | Claims largely unsupported or overstated |
| **Format and audience** | Written for its format and reader; recommendations (if any) follow from the findings | Format followed, but uneven for its reader | Format not followed |
| **Integration and feedback** | One integrated argument; conference feedback visibly addressed | Some drift or repetition; feedback partly addressed | Stapled milestones; feedback ignored |
| **Writing and sources** | Clear and well organized; every citation verified and consistent; format rules met | Minor clarity, citation, or format problems | Hard to follow, or citations missing or unverified |
